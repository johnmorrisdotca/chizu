# Notes on the maps

What the README's [Maps](../README.md#maps) section leaves to this page.

**Japan.** The 47 prefectures are numbered as Japan numbers them, `"1"` (Hokkaidō) to `"47"` (Okinawa), with `iso` the ISO 3166-2 code (`JP-01`); `group` is the region a Japanese school teaches (Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Chubu, Kinki, Chugoku, Shikoku, Kyushu, with Okinawa in Kyushu), Kinki carrying Kansai (関西) in `groupAliases`; the names in both languages and the readings (とうきょうと) are kuni's. The mainland is drawn in Mercator 1,000 across. The islands far from it are drawn in boxes, each holding its own part of the sea (`within`) at a size it can be seen at: Okinawa's main islands large in the Sea of Japan, the Sakishima and Daito Islands beside them, Kagoshima's Tokara and Amami Islands (which Natural Earth draws inside Okinawa and the build gives back) north-east of them, and Tokyo's Ogasawara Islands, Volcano Islands and Minamitorishima at the bottom right.

**What a country alone leaves out.** A country is drawn with its largest piece and every piece within 9° of it, so France is the mainland and Corsica, not French Guiana, Réunion and the Pacific (they are on the world map). The United States is the lower forty-eight and Alaska.

**The world leaves out** Antarctica, the French Southern Lands, Northern Cyprus and Somaliland, and at 1:110m the smallest countries are left off it: 173 are drawn, and every one of the 238 is in its own file. **The world in longitude and latitude** (`@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/world-geo`) is Natural Earth's 1:110m countries as they are, 177 features: the 173 of the flat world and those four, which a globe draws. Of its 177, `code` is the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code, or Natural Earth's three-letter code for Northern Cyprus (`CYN`) and Somaliland (`SOL`), which have none. `pnpm data` writes its data file unformatted, on purpose: formatted it would be about 13,000 lines, larger on disk and in the bundle.

**Finer, in longitude and latitude, for a globe zoomed in.** `@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/world-geo-detail` is Natural Earth's 1:50m countries, 242 features (the 177 of `world-geo` with the same properties, and the 65 smaller countries and territories the 1:110m file leaves off), and `@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/divisions-geo/<code>` is the regions of each of the 32 countries that have them (Natural Earth admin-1 at 1:10m), each feature with `code` (the same as in `divisions/<code>`), `iso`, `name` and `nameJa`. `@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/divisions-geo` has `DIVISIONS_GEO_CODES` and `loadDivisionsGeo(code)`, a dynamic import of one country's file, so a page pays only for the countries it loads and a page that imports none of them pays nothing. They are no projection: no boxes for Alaska, Hawaii or Japan's islands, no canvas. Points are rounded to 0.01 degree (at most about 0.8 km) and each line is simplified by Douglas-Peucker to 0.7 km for the regions and 0.3 km for the world, the most it may lie from Natural Earth's line, on top of the rounding. The border two regions share is cut out as its own line and simplified once, so both regions have the same points on it and a globe can find the borders between regions from them. Packed, gzipped: each country's regions 4 to 172 kB (Russia the largest, then Canada), all 32 together 971 kB, and the 1:50m world 400 kB; the tests hold them under 1.0 MB in all, 200 kB for any one country and 0.45 MB. The data is a string given to `JSON.parse`, not an object literal, which a parser reads faster. `docs/bundle-size.json` lists `./divisions-geo` as one figure because the site's measure bundles every file the entry can import; a page pays for the entry's few hundred bytes and the countries it loads. `scripts/data-config.mjs` has the two tolerances. Natural Earth is in the public domain.
