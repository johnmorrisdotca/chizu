# Notes on the maps

What the README's [Maps](../README.md#maps) section leaves to this page.

**How many regions.** The 33 countries' maps have 1,378 regions in all: among them the 50 states and Washington DC, Canada's 10 provinces and 3 territories, China's 31 provinces and municipalities, Germany's 16 states, Brazil's 27, Mexico's 32, Australia's 10 states and territories, France's 96 départements, India's 36 states and union territories, the United Kingdom's 232 divisions and Japan's 47 prefectures. The dated maps add 34 (`vn-2025`) and 233 (`gb-2025`).

**Japan.** The 47 prefectures are numbered as Japan numbers them, `"1"` (Hokkaidō) to `"47"` (Okinawa), with `iso` the ISO 3166-2 code (`JP-01`); `group` is the region a Japanese school teaches (Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Chubu, Kinki, Chugoku, Shikoku, Kyushu, with Okinawa in Kyushu), Kinki carrying Kansai (関西) in `groupAliases`; the names in both languages and the readings (とうきょうと) are kuni's. The mainland is drawn in Mercator 1,000 across. The islands far from it are drawn in boxes, each holding its own part of the sea (`within`) at a size it can be seen at: Okinawa's main islands large in the Sea of Japan, the Sakishima and Daito Islands beside them, Kagoshima's Tokara and Amami Islands (which Natural Earth draws inside Okinawa and the build gives back) north-east of them, and Tokyo's Ogasawara Islands, Volcano Islands and Minamitorishima at the bottom right.

**India.** `divisions/in` has 36 regions, 28 states and 8 union territories, from Natural Earth 5.1.2's admin-1, which does not predate the changes of 2019 and 2020: Ladakh (`IN-LA`) and Jammu and Kashmir (`IN-JK`) are two union territories, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu is one region (`IN-DH`, in three pieces). It does predate ISO 3166-2:IN's change of 2023-11-23, so a region's `code` is Natural Earth's (`OR`, `CT`, `TG`, `UT`) and its `iso` is the current code, from `ISO_JOIN`: Odisha `IN-OD` (was `IN-OR`), Chhattisgarh `IN-CG` (was `IN-CT`), Telangana `IN-TS` (was `IN-TG`), Uttarakhand `IN-UK` (was `IN-UT`). Natural Earth's own `type_en` is wrong for Himachal Pradesh (it says union territory) and empty for Gujarat, so `TYPE_FIXES` makes both `state`, and a test holds every region's `type` to the official list. The shapes are Natural Earth's de facto lines, with no say on the disputed areas; a point-of-view option is MODULES-1614.

**Dated maps.** Two countries have a second map of their regions, beside their own, which it becomes in the next major version. Vietnam's `vn` is the 63 provinces before 2025-07-01, and `vn-2025` the 34 provinces and cities of current law (Resolution 202/2025/QH15); ISO 3166-2 has no codes yet for the 23 that were merged ([docs/iso-codes.md](./iso-codes.md)). The United Kingdom's `gb` is Natural Earth's councils from before the reforms of 2019 to 2023, and `gb-2025` the councils of 2025 from the ONS, with Northern Ireland as on `gb`. `DIVISIONS_CODES` lists them as `VN-2025` and `GB-2025`; [the loading guide](./guides/loading-maps.md) has both.

**What a country alone leaves out.** A country is drawn with its largest piece and every piece within 9° of it, so France is the mainland and Corsica, not French Guiana, Réunion and the Pacific (they are on the world map). The United States is the lower forty-eight and Alaska.

**The places Natural Earth's countries do not draw alone.** Fifteen ISO 3166-1 codes would have no map of their own from the 1:50m countries: Antarctica and the French Southern Lands are left off them on purpose (`EXCLUDED`), France's overseas departments are inside France, Svalbard and Jan Mayen inside Norway, Christmas and Cocos are one polygon, and Gibraltar, the US Minor Outlying Islands and Bouvet are not at 1:50m. Each is cut from Natural Earth's admin-0 map units (by `GU_A3`) or countries (by `ADM0_A3`), at the scale the rule above gives, so `countries/<code>` and `loadCountry` have all fifteen (`CUT_OUTLINES` in `scripts/data-config/outlines.mjs`). From the map units: French Guiana (`GF`, 1:50m), Svalbard and Jan Mayen together (`SJ`, 1:50m), and at 1:10m Guadeloupe (`GP`), Martinique (`MQ`), Réunion (`RE`), Mayotte (`YT`), Christmas Island (`CX`), the Cocos Islands (`CC`), the Caribbean Netherlands (`BQ`: Bonaire, Saba and Sint Eustatius), Tokelau (`TK`) and Bouvet Island (`BV`). From the countries: Antarctica (`AQ`, 1:50m), and at 1:10m Gibraltar (`GI`, seven points, as Natural Earth draws it), the French Southern Lands (`TF`) and the US Minor Outlying Islands (`UM`). The 9° rule applies to them too, so `TF` is Kerguelen (Crozet, Amsterdam and Saint-Paul and the Scattered Islands are further off), and `UM`, whose islands lie from Navassa in the Caribbean to Wake in the western Pacific, is the three Line Islands round its largest piece: Palmyra Atoll, Kingman Reef and Jarvis Island, each a few units across on a canvas 1,000 tall; Midway, Johnston, Wake, Baker, Howland and Navassa are not on it. `AQ` is the continent and the islands within 9° of it, centred on the South Pole with 0° (Greenwich) up and 90°E to the right, as polar maps draw it. Antarctica's ring runs down the antimeridian to the pole and back, and the build takes those points out so the map has no line from the coast to the pole. None of the fifteen is on the world, in its quiz or in a continent's or subregion's `codes`, and Antarctica and the French Southern Lands are still left off the world. Natural Earth's own `IOA` polygon (Christmas Island with the Cocos Islands) is kept as it was; with `CX` and `CC` drawn alone it is redundant, and taking it out is for a later change.

**The world leaves out** Antarctica, the French Southern Lands, Northern Cyprus and Somaliland, and at 1:110m the smallest countries are left off it: 173 are drawn, and every one of the 253 is in its own file. The 62 it lacks (Singapore, Malta, Bahrain, most of the Caribbean and the Pacific; not Ashmore and Cartier, the Indian Ocean Territories or Siachen) can be added as dots, each a region with `small: true` at the middle of its largest island or piece, by `withSmallStates(WORLD)` from `@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/small-states`, its own entry so the table costs nothing until it is used. Give the result to `drawChizu`, `findQuestion` or `pickDistractors`; `mountChizu` (`smallStates: true`), `<chizu-map small-states>` and `<chizu-quiz small-states>` fetch the entry themselves. Nothing turns it on by itself, so a seeded quiz asks what it always did. They are made at run time from a table of longitude and latitude read from the 1:50m outlines (`world-geo-detail`), so `WORLD` stays 173 regions and the generated data is unchanged. **The world in longitude and latitude** (`@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/world-geo`) is Natural Earth's 1:110m countries as they are, 177 features: the 173 of the flat world and those four, which a globe draws. Of its 177, `code` is the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code, or Natural Earth's three-letter code for Northern Cyprus (`CYN`) and Somaliland (`SOL`), which have none. `pnpm data` writes its data file unformatted, on purpose: formatted it would be about 13,000 lines, larger on disk and in the bundle.

**Finer, in longitude and latitude, for a globe zoomed in.** `@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/world-geo-detail` is Natural Earth's 1:50m countries, 242 features (the 177 of `world-geo` with the same properties, and the 65 smaller countries and territories the 1:110m file leaves off), and `@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/divisions-geo/<code>` is the regions of each of the 33 countries that have them (Natural Earth admin-1 at 1:10m), and of the two dated maps, `vn-2025` and `gb-2025`, each feature with `code` (the same as in `divisions/<code>`), `iso`, `name` and `nameJa`. `@johnmorrisdotca/chizu/divisions-geo` has `DIVISIONS_GEO_CODES` and `loadDivisionsGeo(code)`, a dynamic import of one country's file, so a page pays only for the countries it loads and a page that imports none of them pays nothing. They are no projection: no boxes for Alaska, Hawaii or Japan's islands, no canvas. Points are rounded to 0.01 degree (at most about 0.8 km) and each line is simplified by Douglas-Peucker to 0.7 km for the regions and 0.3 km for the world, the most it may lie from Natural Earth's line, on top of the rounding. The border two regions share is cut out as its own line and simplified once, so both regions have the same points on it and a globe can find the borders between regions from them. Every ring is cut at the same set of points, and the build refuses to simplify to more edges that a region has and its neighbour does not share than the outlines have when they are only rounded. Packed, gzipped: each country's regions 4 to 172 kB (Russia the largest, then Canada), all 33 together 1,016 kB, and the 1:50m world 400 kB; the tests hold them under 1.1 MB in all, 200 kB for any one country and 0.45 MB. The dated maps, 14 kB (`vn-2025`) and 52 kB (`gb-2025`), are held to the 200 kB of any one file and are not in the 33 countries' total, since a page loads one or the other of a country's maps. The data is a string given to `JSON.parse`, not an object literal, which a parser reads faster. `docs/bundle-size.json` lists `./divisions-geo` as one figure because the site's measure bundles every file the entry can import; a page pays for the entry's few hundred bytes and the countries it loads. `scripts/data-config.mjs` has the two tolerances. Natural Earth is in the public domain.
